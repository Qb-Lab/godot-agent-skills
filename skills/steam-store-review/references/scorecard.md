# Conversion scorecard — anchors

Scores only mean something if they mean the same thing next month. Each axis below gives what
a **3**, a **6**, and a **9** look like. Score against the anchors, not against a feeling, and
quote the evidence you looked at — the ladder file, the grid screenshot, the timestamp in the
trailer.

A 10 is reserved for an element that would work for a game with ten times the budget. Most
good pages score 7s. A page of 6s converts; a page with one 3 in the first four axes usually
does not, because the funnel is sequential — a 9 screenshot cannot rescue a 3 capsule, since
nobody reaches it.

## Capsule click potential

- **3** — reads as noise in the grid; no single focal point survives the listing size.
- **6** — stops the eye but could be three other games in the genre; the subject is clear, the
  hook is not.
- **9** — one unmistakable shape, inferable genre and tone, and a reason to click that the
  neighbours do not have.

Judge in a real competitor grid screenshot at listing size. "Looks good" at full resolution is
not evidence for this axis.

## Logo readability

- **3** — the wordmark is mush at 184x69, or needs a drop shadow to survive its own background.
- **6** — readable at the smallest size, but generic — a display font applied as-is.
- **9** — readable at 184x69, in greyscale and blurred, with a silhouette that is recognisable
  before it is read and type that says something true about the game.

Evidence is the ladder output, not the source file.

## Short-description clarity

- **3** — lore-first; after one read a stranger cannot say what they would do.
- **6** — genre and action are present but buried past the first clause, or generic enough to
  fit three competitors.
- **9** — genre, verb, and hook in one pass, in this game's own nouns, and the first clause
  works alone because somewhere it will appear alone.

## Trailer hook

- **3** — studio logo, fade-in, or an establishing shot before anything happens.
- **6** — gameplay starts early but the most interesting thing arrives after fifteen seconds.
- **9** — cold open on the single most interesting second, four or five distinct highlights
  inside the first fifteen, and it still works muted.

## Screenshot strength

- **3** — fewer than five, or several doing the same job; a menu or a cinematic frame in the set.
- **6** — every slot filled with real gameplay, but the first shot is a vista rather than the
  loop, or two images share a palette and a composition.
- **9** — each shot has a distinct, nameable job; the first carries the core loop or fantasy;
  visible variety in palette, environment, and composition across the set.

## Gameplay communication

- **3** — a stranger who saw the whole page still cannot describe a minute of play.
- **6** — they can describe the activity but not what makes it interesting.
- **9** — they can describe the loop *and* the tension in it, from the page alone.

Test it by writing the sentence a stranger would say. If you cannot write it from the page
without using knowledge from `GAME.md`, this axis is below 6.

## Visual consistency

- **3** — the capsule promises a different game than the screenshots deliver. This is the one
  that converts into refunds and "nothing like the trailer" reviews.
- **6** — same game, but the capsule is noticeably more polished than the build.
- **9** — capsule, trailer, screenshots, and logo read as one art direction, and the capsule is
  an honest best moment rather than an upgrade.

## Store differentiation

- **3** — the page would fit any of the five nearest comparables with the title swapped.
- **6** — the wedge exists in the copy but not in the visuals, or the reverse.
- **9** — the thing that makes this game worth making is visible in the capsule, the first
  screenshot, and the first clause of the description.

Requires a current market brief. Without one, score this `unverified` rather than guessing —
differentiation is a claim about other games, and `market-scan`'s rule applies.

## Professional polish

- **3** — emoji headers, buzzword stacks, templated phrasing, visible generation tells.
- **6** — clean and competent but anonymous; nothing wrong, nothing authored.
- **9** — reads as written by someone who cares about this specific game, in a voice, with no
  filler.

## Wishlist motivation

- **3** — nothing to come back for; the page is a description, not an invitation.
- **6** — interesting, but there is no particular reason to act now rather than forget.
- **9** — a specific unanswered curiosity, a clear reason to want the finished thing, and a
  visible next step (demo, date, follow).

## Using the scores

- Report the table, then the **one** biggest weakness and the **one** highest-leverage fix.
  The table is diagnosis; two lines are the prescription.
- Re-score after a fix and show both numbers. A page that went 5 → 8 on the capsule and lost a
  point on consistency is information the user needs.
- The lowest score among the first four axes is usually the one to act on, because the funnel
  is sequential. Say when it is not, and why.
